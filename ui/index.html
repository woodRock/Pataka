Start a kōrero. It replaces them with placeholders, and replies are restored here, on your device.

      Ngā mātāpono: the principles behind Pātaka
      
        
      Pātaka is a storehouse. Taonga kept in a pātaka were raised up and protected, and what went in or out was decided by those responsible for it. This app tries to treat your data the same way. It draws on the Te Mana Raraunga principles of Māori data sovereignty and the CARE principles for Indigenous data governance.
      
        
        
          
      • Rangatiratanga (authority). You decide what leaves. Data is stored only on this device, encrypted, with no account, telemetry or sync. Tapu kōrero cannot be sent off the device.
        • 
          
      • Whakapapa (provenance). Each message records which model and provider handled it, and where that provider processes data. You can see exactly what left the device.
        • 
          
      • Whanaungatanga and kotahitanga (collective interests). Affiliation (iwi, hapū, marae, whānau) is treated as identifying, because in small communities it is. Kaitiaki lists let a community set what is protected.
        • 
          
      • Manaakitanga (care). Tūmataiti redacts before sending, and the review step shows you the outbound text in full.
        • 
          
      • Kaitiakitanga (guardianship). A tamper-evident audit log records what kinds of data went where, without storing the data itself. You can export or delete everything at any time.
        • 
        
      
        
      Redaction reduces risk; it does not remove it. Tūmataiti's rules find structured identifiers, and its name model finds most names, addresses and affiliation, but no detector catches everything: read what will be sent. Decisions about mātauranga belong to the people it comes from: the tool flags, people decide.
